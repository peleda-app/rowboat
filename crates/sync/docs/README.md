# rowboat-sync

Journal replication and commands between a person's nodes ([GROUP.md](../../../docs/GROUP.md), "Journal replication" and "Commands from any node"). A library: storage (Skein's `Storage`), the link (`baton_link::Link`), signals (`baton_signal::Signal`) and what the node knows about itself (`Host`) arrive as contracts; time arrives as a `rowboat_group::Now`. In wave 1 the owner of an account is the node that added it; every other node with `read` follows its journal and sends it commands.

## Heads and attestation

- `NodeAttestor` is Skein's `HeadAttestor` for the node's own journal: every head it writes carries `attest(node key, head)`, a versioned postcard value with the signer's key and its Ed25519 signature over `JournalHead::digest()` under the context `rowboat-head-v1`. Reading its own storage it accepts a head without attestation (journals from before attestation) and a head signed by any node the group log ever certified (a revoked former owner's history stays readable), and refuses everything else. This is deliberately more lenient than Skein's `head_attestor` suite, which refuses unattested heads; the strict check is `verify_remote`.
- `verify_remote(membership, head)`: a head from a peer must be well formed, attested, signed by a node that counts in this node's group log now and holds `lead` on `acct-{account}`, with a fence at most 5 minutes ahead of this node's clock, and a grant (when it carries one) that `Grant::check` accepts. Returns the signer.
- **Grants** (`Grant`, `GrantKind`; question 79, GROUP.md "When the server is silent"): the arbiter that gave a lease signs `(kind, arbiter, group, key, node, fence)` under `rowboat-grant-v1`, at most 512 bytes. `Grant::check` accepts it for a head only when it is for this group, this account, the head's signer and exactly the head's fence, and the arbiter counts now and is of its kind's class (`Server` for a server node, `Fallback` for a desktop or phone), so a revoked arbiter's grants never pass, even ones signed before. `NodeAttestor::set_grants` gives the attestor a `Grants` source; from then on every head is attested in version 2 (`attest_granted`: the signature under `rowboat-head-v2` covers the head's digest and the grant's hash, so a grant can be neither swapped nor stripped), carrying the grant the node holds for that account and fence. Version 1 attestations (no grant) still verify. `head_grant` and `attestation_grant` read the grant of a verified head. Attestations are at most 1 KiB.

## The head protocol (`heads`, link protocol `head`, `peleda/head/1` on iroh)

`Ask { accounts }` is answered with `Heads`: the signed head of every named account the asking node may read in the group log and this node holds (replicas included), each with the owner's `About` (address, display name, archive mailbox; display only) when this node leads it. `Announce` pushes an owner's new head; the receiver acknowledges at once and verifies afterwards. At most 256 accounts per message, 256 KiB per frame; every message has one canonical encoding.

## Peers, the peer tier and what is served

- `Peers`: who to ask for an account's objects, owner (signer of the newest verified head, while it counts) first, then every counting node that may read it; the accounts this node may read; the `baton-fetch` fetcher once the link is up.
- `PeerTier` is a Skein `ReadTier` over `baton-fetch`: a segment is fetched under its account resource by its hash, with its name as the fetch locator (B5); a blob under each account resource this node reads until one peer has it. Without a link it has nothing. The node puts it behind its local storage with `skein-storage-tiered`, so the view and `message_raw` read blobs from peers the node does not hold.
- `Served` is what the fetch server hands out, always from the node's own local storage (never through its tiers): a segment named by the locator, only when its bytes hash to the requested id; a blob only when `Host::holds_blob` says it belongs to the account it was asked under (a message or object of it in the view, or the blob of a command this node issued). Who may ask for a resource is the fetch server's `authorize`: `read` in the group log. Anything else is "not here".

## Replication

`Replication::round` asks every counting peer for the heads of the readable accounts that did not fork (led ones too, so a leader learns of a later holder); `Announce` brings single heads, and `hear(peer, heads)` considers heads as if `peer` announced them. Every verified head is then put to `Host::admits` with the grant behind its fence and the grant behind this node's own head (the node's rule is GROUP.md's; the default admits everything); a head refused there is ignored like a stale one, and an offer carrying it is refused. An admitted head of an account this node leads with a fence above its own head goes to `Host::superseded` (the node gives the account up) and is not followed while it still leads. For each account the newest verified head (by fence, then `seq`) is followed; a stale head (lower fence or `seq`, a replay) changes nothing. Following a head:

1. The local head and the new one are compared (`check_successor`): a lower fence or `seq` is stale, a different tip at the same `seq` is a fork.
2. Segments are walked back from the new tip through the tiered log (local, then peers, owner first), each verified against its reference (hash, header, link, events), and staged with Skein's `Log::stage` until the walk reaches the local tip (or the first event for an empty replica). A segment at or below the local head that is not the local tip is a fork.
3. Blobs of the new `message_added`, `object_upserted` and `itip_received` events (at most 4,096 per catch-up) are fetched into local storage first, so the view never parses a message before its bytes are here. If some are missing, the head waits; after three tries it is installed anyway.
4. `Log::install` moves the head (storing the owner's attestation verbatim), the `heads/{account}` signal wakes `skein-view`, and `Host::installed` is told.

A fork stops following the account for good (until `clear_fork`), keeps both heads' digests at `rowboat/forks/{account}` and tells `Host::forked`. A head under a higher fence that does not continue the local journal (it continues an earlier head, or rolls back below the local `seq`) is not a fork: this node wrote while it had been deposed, so `Caught::Deposed` drops the replica (`Host::reset`) and the staged offer, and the next round follows from the start. A head under a lower fence is stale. Replication never installs over an account the node leads. `drop_replica(account)` drops a replica the same way on request (a journal this node wrote that the group did not take), never one the node leads.

## Handover (`handover`, link protocol `handover`)

`export` collects an account's state from the node's control store (Skein's per-account records and trees, `rowboat/accounts/{account}`, `rowboat/cmdkeys/{account}/...`, credentials opened with the node's keyring, and the queued commands), reads the head last and seals it all (XChaCha20-Poly1305 through `baton-seal`) under the account resource's current **secret key**, bound to the head's digest, the epoch and the account, at most 32 MiB and 100,000 records. `Replication::hand_over` pushes the `Offer` to every counting node that may lead the account. `receive_offer` takes it only when the sender counts and holds `lead`, this node may lead and does not lead the account, the head verifies and was signed by the sender, the offer is not below the one held (fence, then `seq`; the same offer again is accepted) nor below the local head's fence, and the local replica reached the head; it stores the offer sealed (`rowboat/handover/{account}`) and writes the account record so the node may compete for the lease. Only `import`, called by the next holder when it takes the lease, opens it: the account's records are replaced by the offer's, credentials sealed again under the importing node's keyring, and the queue made the offer's. `staged` returns the offer held. Commands in the outbox for an account this node now leads are handled locally, except one this node already sent to the previous owner without an answer while `Host::previous_owner_silent` says that owner went silent: it settles `Uncertain` (the owner may have run it).

## Commands

The issuer (`submit`, `cancel`, `sync_now`) wraps the request (`commands::RemoteRequest`: a Skein command for flags, move, delete and send; a cancel; a sync) in a Baton command (`baton-command`) for `acct-{account}`, sealed under the current epoch's data key, valid 24 hours (a sync one hour), with a fresh ULID as idempotency key. The Skein command's id is `derive_queue_id(issuer, resource, key)`: the key's timestamp and 80 bits of a keyed hash, so owner and issuer agree on it without telling each other, two issuers never share one, and the owner's journal events about it settle the issuer's overlay and sends exactly like a local command's. The command waits in a durable outbox (`rowboat/outbox/{id}` in the control store, at most 10,000) and is delivered over the link protocol `command` to the owner until a valid signed result comes back, the owner refuses it, or it expires (then it settles as `Failed { expired }`).

The owner (`Host::leads`) checks every command with `Command::check` against its own group log (signature, group and resource, time window, the issuer counting and holding the class, the payload opening; a command naming a newer log head, or sealed under a key epoch the owner does not know yet, is answered "later", and in the second case the owner asks the node to exchange logs with the issuer, `Host::group_behind`), then its own rules: the request's class is the command's, the Skein command is for this account under the derived id and passes Skein's `check_command`, and a send comes from the account's own address. It claims `rowboat/cmdkeys/{account}/{id}` with put-if-absent before queueing; a redelivery is answered from that record (queued: applied; refused: the same reason; claimed but not queued: the command again when it is repeatable, `Uncertain` otherwise; a run still under way here: "later"), so a command reaches Skein's queue once even after it finished. A send whose blob the owner lacks is answered "later" at once and recorded `fetching`; the blob is fetched from the issuer (then the account's readers) in the background, and when it arrives the owner queues the send itself. A failed fetch is transient: the next delivery starts another. A cancel is run only for a send the cancelling node issued (its `cmdkey` record names it and is `queued`); anything else is `not_queued`. Results are signed (`CommandResult`) and checked by the issuer; a refusal before a command opens travels as a plain reason over the authenticated link. `Applied` means queued: the execution's outcome reaches the issuer through the replicated journal. `prune` drops `cmdkey` records older than two days.

## Limits

| What | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| accounts per head message | 256 |
| head frame | 256 KiB |
| command frame | `baton_command::MAX_ENCODED` + 1 KiB |
| outbox | 10,000 commands |
| blobs fetched per catch-up | 4,096 |
| a fetched object's time | `peer_timeout` (20 s) plus its size at 64 KiB/s |
| owners remembered | 4,096 |
| concurrent streams served per protocol | 8 |
| `cmdkey` records | kept two days, at most 10,000 looked at per prune |

## Tests

`cargo test -p rowboat-sync` (in-process links, memory storage, a manual clock): a reader follows the owner byte for byte with its blobs, by rounds and by announcements; replayed, lower, unsigned, re-signed and foreign heads change nothing; heads of a node without `lead` or of a revoked node are refused; a fork stops following and keeps evidence; a hostile peer serving flipped, truncated, reordered, oversized or another account's segments is skipped and remembered as a liar; a node without `read` gets no segment, blob or head; commands reach the owner's queue once (redelivered, after completion), forged and expired ones are refused, only the owner runs them, the same key from two issuers makes two commands and another issuer's queue id is refused; commands without the class or from another group are refused; a send carries its blob from the issuer and must come from the account's address; a send's blob is fetched after the answer, a stalled fetch never settles the send and a redelivery meanwhile waits; a node cancels only its own queued sends; a permanent delete needs `mail-delete-forever`; an owner behind on the group log answers later and asks to catch up; an expired outbox entry settles failed; the outbox survives a restart; old `cmdkey` records are pruned; an offer moves the state sealed until the next holder opens it (credentials sealed again under its own keyring, the queue and records replaced, importing twice harmless); offers relayed by a node that did not write the head, replayed under a lower head, moved under another head, bit-flipped, offered to the holder, sent by a revoked node, or made by a node without credentials are refused; a deposed writer drops its tail (not a fork) and its heads are stale elsewhere; a command delivered to an owner that never answered settles uncertain when the issuer takes over from that silent owner, and runs when it takes over normally; grants round trip, their every field is signed, and a grant for another fence, group, account or node, a desktop's grant claiming to be a server's, an arbiter that does not count, a grant swapped or stripped under the signer's signature and an oversized grant are refused. Decoders have property tests and the `sync_wire` fuzz target in the root `fuzz/` workspace.
